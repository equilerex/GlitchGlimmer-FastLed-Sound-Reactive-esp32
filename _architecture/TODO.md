## Context

ESP32 sound-reactive LED firmware (PlatformIO, board `ttgo-t1`, FastLED for LEDs on pins 25/33, TFT_eSPI on 18/19/5/16/23, INMP441 I2S mic on 26/27/32). The codebase was largely AI-generated before current models, and inherited the defects of that era.

`plans/2026-09-16-ai-era-defect-audit.md` holds the audit. `plans/2026-09-16-verification-harness-and-what-it-found.md` holds the current measured state and supersedes parts of the audit, so read it first if you only read one.

The correction matters and is worth stating plainly. The audit called the per-frame layer leak the crash, but `SceneDirector::attachState` had no call sites, so `state` was always null, every director call early-returned, `getActiveScene()` returned null, and the per-strip block that built those layers never ran. The leak was unreachable, and with it the whole scene path. Nothing was drawn at all. So the leak is not the crash cause, and whatever crashed on hardware was not this revision.

The user's real symptom, layers stacking until the animation disappears, came from the compositor instead. Every layer renders additively into a black buffer, and `renderLayers` blended that buffer over the base with `nblend(..., 255)`, where 255 means replace rather than blend. Each layer's unlit pixels therefore erased the base animation. Compositing is now additive with opacity as the layer's share of the light.

Wiring the state also uncovered a crash that had been unreachable for the same reason. `animationCatalog` is sized `AnimationType::COUNT`, which is 9, but only 8 entries were initialised and `NONE` is 0. Every lookup reads one position early, and the last slot was left value-initialised with an empty `std::function` that aborts on call. Any scene built from `ALIEN_PULSE` would have called it. This is the one defect found so far that plausibly explains a crash on hardware, and it only became reachable once scenes actually ran.

Where it stands now. Both environments build and the harness runs 142 checks green. `pio run -e ttgo-t1` succeeds at RAM 8.9 percent and flash 32.8 percent at `-std=gnu++11`. `pio run -e native -t exec` drives the real controller over a test-owned clock, sweeps all eight catalog animations at two lengths and all eight reachable layer factories, soaks the four accumulator sites for 20000 frames each, runs the real FFT through `AudioProcessor`, and round-trips a capture file through the same pipeline the browser writes.

The loop no longer has a throw path. Both history buffers were `std::deque`, which took and returned a 456-byte and a 480-byte node about fifteen times a second, and a failed `push_back` was the one way `loop()` could reach `std::terminate`. They are now `SnapshotRing`, a fixed-capacity ring in `src/audio/SnapshotRing.h`, and `AudioHistoryTracker::addSnapshot` allocates zero times across a whole run. `AudioHistory` is 60000 bytes of heap taken once at boot, which is more than anything reads; `BACKLOG.md` carries the shrink.

Nine further defects are fixed, among them self-transitioning scenes, C++20 designated initializers under `gnu++11`, a function-local static scratch buffer shared across strips, and six unwrapped float phase accumulators. One defect surfaced while fixing those and is recorded rather than fixed: `WormholeVortexLayer` and `CentroidColorFlowLayer` are never instantiated anywhere. Nothing has run on hardware, and that is the open item. It is also worth repeating that the harness's allocation counts exclude `String`, because `sim/stubs/Arduino.h` aliases it to `std::string` and small-string optimisation hides the churn the device would pay.

The repository was then made buildable by someone who is not this machine's owner, and given somewhere to look at the output. Three of the four libraries `src/` includes were reaching the build through a `lib_extra_dirs` absolute path into a global Arduino libraries directory, so a fresh clone found only FastLED. All four are now named in `lib_deps`, which also means the TFT_eSPI on the build machine and the one CI downloads are the same version. TFT_eSPI's display settings followed the same problem one level down, since they lived in the library's own `User_Setup_Select.h` rather than in the repo. `TFT_eSPI.h` checks `__has_include(<tft_setup.h>)` before it reads that file, so `include/tft_setup.h` now carries the panel configuration and wins. Both environments build from a clean tree with nothing but PlatformIO installed.

`web/` has two views over one canvas. The recording view replays frames the harness writes with `--dump-frames`, so its audio timeline is fixed and nothing there runs the FFT. The live view is the same `animations/` and `scenes/` code compiled to WebAssembly by `tools/build-wasm.sh` and stepped once per frame from the page, with the microphone's samples handed to `AudioProcessor` unchanged, so the FFT, the feature extraction, the beat detector and the mood classifier are the firmware's own. It opens on the microphone and stays there: the synthetic signal is selectable and is never a fallback, because the analysis keeps statistics of whatever was playing and a fallback silently measures the microphone against the other signal's references. `?debug=1` turns on a console trace of every scene and mood change with its dwell time, retained on `window.ggTrace`.

The live view reads its state back out of the module every frame and shows it: the mood now, the mood of the averaged window, the scene clock against its own thresholds, and one bar per classifier input with the threshold marked on it. That panel is what turned "the animations keep blacking out" from an impression into a number. Before the feature-scale fix, the synthetic signal left strip 0's summed channels under 2000 of a possible 76500, lit at around two percent. The cause was the feature scale, not the compositor: `energy` is a raw FFT magnitude sum and five separate comparisons treated it as 0..1, and the bass, mid and treble divisors were small enough that broadband audio pinned all three at 1.0. With the sweep done the same panel reads all 100 pixels of strip 0 non-black at 5600 to 9500 summed channels, so roughly seven to twelve percent of the strip's capacity rather than two.

That scale problem is now fixed at the producer. `AudioFeatures::level` is the block's volume as a fraction of the loudest block in the last few seconds, so it is 0..1 at any microphone gain and needs no device constant, and `MoodHistory::classifyMood`, the classifier copy in `SceneRegistry::pickSceneByMood`, `SceneState::shouldTransition` and `calculateIdealDuration` all read it. The gate's thresholds became multiples of the noise floor for the same reason, since a fixed threshold was a claim about one microphone's output and this one runs about 40 dB below it, which left the gate shut through music. `AlienPulse` and `BassPulseStorm` were rendering black off the same class of constant and now drive off `level`. `bassHits` was never assigned anywhere in the repo, so three consumer branches were dead.

The producer fix was not enough on its own, because the consumers were still comparing against absolute magnitudes. Every remaining `.energy`, `.volume` and `.loudness` read in `src/` was reviewed and the comparisons moved onto `level`: `NeonBeatTunnelAnimation` was black because it multiplied a 0..255 wave by a volume near 0.008, `PsychedelicInkSquirtAnimation` had an `energy * 0.05` term contributing 5 to 50 units a second against two musical terms of about 2, `EnergyPulseRiverLayer` moved a river hundreds of pixels a frame, `EnergyFogLayer` sat at its brightness ceiling on every frame, `MoodMemoryArcLayer` and `CentroidColorFlowLayer` read the same wrong scale, and `SceneDirector::maybeInjectReactiveLayer` had a `> 0.6f` test that was true on every frame and so injected an overlay on its 1500 ms timer regardless of the audio. `DisplayManager`'s power bar and power readout were reading `loudness`, which is `volume * 100`, so both showed zero through music. `MoodReactiveAnimation.h` was dead and unbuildable, referencing mood types that do not exist, and is deleted. The remaining absolute uses are the producer, the snapshot plumbing and the harness, none of which compares one against a threshold.

Two defects were fixed on the strength of that same review. `SceneState::shouldTransition` compared the instantaneous `level` against the snapshot taken when the scene began, and `level` is renormalised to the loudest recent block with about 11 percent per-frame churn, so the 0.25 delta was crossed within a second or two of any real audio and `pastMin && moodShift` was satisfied the moment the minimum elapsed. Every scene therefore changed at its minimum duration, which is the reported bug. It now compares the classified mood, which is already smoothed, confirmed and held, and the bpm term is unchanged. Second, the mood's fixed thresholds were a claim about one input's scale: on the microphone in use `dynamics`, then a crest factor, lived between about 0.46 and 0.57, so `> 0.5` was true on nearly every frame and `< 0.2` never. The cut points are now 30 and 70 percent of the range the classifier has measured, with the dynamics clause dropped entirely when that range is too narrow to split. A mood also now holds for a minimum dwell, and a competing mood must persist for a confirmation window before it may replace it, with the UNKNOWN to real transition exempt from both, since a held mood to outlast exists only once a real one has been chosen.

All of that is adjustable live. The page's Tuning group drives eight dials over one indexed wasm pair: the scene clock's minimum base, ideal base and ideal span, and the mood's minimum hold, confirmation window, smoothing rate and two dynamics-window rates. They persist to `localStorage` and have a reset. The two dynamics-window rates are per second rather than per frame, so the same window adapts at the same wall-clock speed on the device's 33 ms loop and the page's per-frame loop. The panel's dynamics markers are placed from the firmware's own cut points rather than a constant, and hidden when the measured range is too narrow to split.

The loop for tuning against the real microphone is `Record` on the live page, which writes what the page fed the analyser to a `.f32` file, then `.pio/build/native/program --replay <file>` (`.exe` on Windows, and `pio run -e native` has to have produced it once), which feeds it back through the real `AudioProcessor` and reports the min, max, mean and frame-to-frame churn of every feature plus the mood dwell times. The binary is named directly because `pio run -t exec` takes no program arguments and rejects them as stray options, which is a mistake three separate comments in `web/live.js` had been recommending. The churn column is what separates a value inside a sane range from one inside a sane range and unusable, and the format is verified end to end by `?selftest=1` on the page against `checkReplay` in the harness. The browser is still the only place the microphone is reachable, so this is the only route from a real input to a measurement.

`tools/build-wasm.sh` compiles to cached objects under `web/live/.obj` and rebuilds in about 20 seconds after a source or repo-header change, against about 85 seconds cold. Pass `--watch` to rebuild on save. The residual cost is the link of 135 objects, not the compile: a no-change run still takes 15 seconds. Archiving FastLED's objects so the linker pulls only what is referenced was tried and made it slower, so it is not in the script.

The animations were still reading near black at the microphone's level after the feature-scale fix, and the cause turned out to be in FastLED rather than here. `hsv2rgb_rainbow` squares `CHSV`'s `val` before it scales the channels, `val = scale8_video(val, val)`, so `CHSV(h, s, v)` emits duty proportional to `v * v`. A `sqrt` brightness curve on that input cancels exactly, and the catalog animations measured at a ratio of exactly `level` against a full-level run, which is that quadratic response written as one number. `AudioFeatures` now carries a curve per colour sink: `pixelLevel()` for brightness written as a scale on `CRGB`, and `hsvLevel()` for brightness written through `CHSV`'s `val`, which supplies the other half of the exponent so both sinks land on the same emitted duty. The tunnel's brightest channel at the microphone's level went from 40 to 98 of 255 and its measured ratio from 0.162 to 0.399, which is `sqrt(0.159)` to three places. A harness check measures the conversion itself rather than the arithmetic, so a FastLED upgrade that changes the squaring fails there instead of as a strip that has quietly gone dark.

`level` was the other half of that report, and it was not a level. It was the current block over the loudest recent one with nothing after it, so any block louder than the one before set it to 1 for a single frame and the value fell away again by the next. At roughly 86 blocks a second that is a spike train, and it is what the readout was showing. Two things were wrong and only the first was visible.

The spike train came from measuring a room with an 11.6 ms window. A block's RMS is not the loudness of the surroundings, because on music it swings by a factor of two or more between one block and the next, so the ratio moved with the beat and `level` answered "did something just hit" rather than "how loud is this". `level` is now a fraction of `levelEnv`, an asymmetric one-pole envelope of the block volume with separate attack and release, so a kick is a few percent of the way up its range rather than at the top, and the reference is the loudest thing that envelope has reached, forgetting over about twenty seconds so a quiet passage stays quiet. That slowness is the term that decides which of the two questions the value answers. The second fix is that it is normalised against the noise floor as well as the peak, so silence reads as zero by construction rather than as a ratio of two falling terms that climbs back toward 1 and was only hidden by the gate.

"There is no filter on the ratio itself now, the envelope and the reference are the filters" was the claim, and the microphone showed it was wrong twice over. Both terms were slow relative to a block and neither was slow relative to a word. Attack 0.05 and release 0.02 per block are about half a second and a second, and the gap between two words is a few hundred milliseconds, so the envelope crossed most of the way down to the room level between them and most of the way back up on the next syllable. The reading still swung from zero to the top of its range inside a tenth of a second, and the report was that the level was a beat detector again under a new name. The coefficients are 0.02 and 0.006 now, which puts attack below the timescale a syllable moves at and release above the one a room does. Two further terms were in the same report. The reference rose onto a louder envelope instantly, so a single cough set the denominator for the next twenty seconds and everything after it read dim, and it now rises at 0.05 a block, which takes a sustained passage rather than one block. The silence gate ramped at 0.12 a block, and `level` is multiplied by that gate, so the reading fell off a cliff at the end of every phrase however slow the envelope was. The gate is 0.04. All four numbers are in `Config.h` with the reasoning beside them. The mood thresholds were tuned against the unfiltered value and still pass, because the classifier smooths, confirms and holds the mood before acting on it.

The beat detector carried the same class of defect as the gate, in two places, and the tempo readout was the reported one. Its threshold was `max(0.008f, previousVolume * 0.35f)`, and that 0.008 is a claim about one microphone's output like any other: the microphone in use peaks near 0.02, so a third of the entire range was a bar that any block carrying room noise cleared, and the tempo followed the room. The floor is now a multiple of the measured noise floor and the fraction against the previous block stays, because a single 11.6 ms window is the right reference for an onset and anything slower already carries the beat it is supposed to detect. The second defect was the readout rather than the detection: `60000.0 / sinceBeat` assigned from the interval that had just elapsed, so a beat landing a block early or late moved the number by tens of BPM and one the detector missed doubled the interval and halved it. `currentBPM` is now the median of the last twelve intervals, which discards both, and the window is dropped when the tempo decays to zero so the first beat after a break is not averaged against a tempo that stopped a minute ago. A harness check drives a steady 800 ms train with one gap twice that long and asserts the reading either side of the gap is the tempo and not what the gap implies.

Neither of those is what the room was reporting, which was 140 to 200 BPM while someone walked and spoke with no beat playing. The reason is that a level rise is not a beat and speech is almost nothing but level rises. Every syllable starts on a consonant, the block before it was quieter, so the detector fired on nearly every word, and the 250 ms refractory capped the tempo in the 140 to 240 range rather than stopping it. A beat is a low-frequency transient and a consonant is not, so a block now has to clear a third condition, that the bass band under 200 Hz rose to 1.15 times what it was on the block before. That comparison is against the band's raw magnitude and not its share, and the first version of it got that wrong. A share of a block that is mostly bass is already at the top of its range, so a share cannot rise, and the condition could never fire on bass-heavy material, which the harness fixture turned out to be. The raw magnitude has no ceiling. It is read before the gate as well, since the gate opening is itself a rise. The one thing this depends on is bass in the room, and a source with no low end under 200 Hz has no low-frequency transient to find. That is worth knowing before it is read as a bug, and the harness fixture is a 172 Hz tone, which is inside the band.

The other two numbers in that report were wrong by construction rather than by tuning. `mid` sat at the top of its bar at all times, and that is what the band set did: the three shares were rescaled against the largest of them, so the dominant band read exactly 1.0 whatever was playing, and on this microphone the dominant band is mid for anything carrying a voice or a melody. The rescale is gone. The three are shares, which is what they already were before it, and their proportions are now whatever the audio has, so a mid-heavy passage reads mid 0.65 against bass 0.03 and treble 0.17 rather than mid pinned and the other two moving. The cost is that every band reads lower than it did, by about the factor the rescale was lifting it, so an animation driving brightness from a band needs its own scale. Inflating a measurement to suit a consumer is what produced the pinned band, so that scale belongs in the animation.

That scale turned out not to belong in the animation either, because the animations it would have gone into were rendering near black on real audio while passing every check. The harness fixture fed the bands at up to 0.95, twenty times the 0.049 real bass produces, so it was the only place the absolute band constants in those animations were ever satisfied, and the catalog's brightness check was ratio-only: an animation dim at both the quiet and the loud fixture reads a ratio of 1.0 and passes, which a strip that is black at both also does. The bands are still the honest measurement, and each now has a drive beside it. `bassLevel`, `midLevel` and `trebleLevel` are each band against its own recent peak, the same 0..1 question `level` asks of loudness, gated by the same silence gate and held between blocks in one `BandRefs` member next to `levelRef`. One reference per band and not the single shared peak the shares were once rescaled against, because the bands do not peak together. The animations read the drives and the constants are gone. On the microphone the shares are bass 0.001 to 0.049, mid 0.033 to 0.70 and treble 0.006 to 0.40, while the drives sit at 0.04 to 0.63, 0.04 to 0.81 and 0.04 to 0.82, and `BassPulseStorm`'s peak channel at the microphone's level went from 42 of 255 to 168. The fixture's band targets were moved to the measured share scale, and a new check asserts an absolute floor rather than a ratio, that every catalog animation reaches channel 64 of 255 on real band shares. It was falsified by reverting one animation to its old absolute constant, which failed it.

The same fixture problem had a second face, and it is why the microphone looked broken rather than the animations. The page's synthetic signal was generated at full scale, so it produced `volume` 0.3 and `peak` 0.85 against the microphone's 0.006 and 0.017, and every absolute reading on the page was fifty times what the same page showed on the microphone. Nothing in the render path reads those three any more, or `loudness`, and none of them ever needed to: they are absolute sample amplitudes and scale with the microphone's gain, while `level`, `pixelLevel` and the band drives are fractions of a recent reference and read the same on any input. The demo generator now carries a `DEMO_GAIN` of 0.02 and lands at volume 0.003 to 0.012, peak 0.006 to 0.024 and average 0.002 to 0.011, which is the microphone's own range, so the two sources agree about every reading. The three rows in the panel lost their bars, since there is no maximum to draw them against, and `AudioFeatures::agcLevel` was deleted: it was declared, defaulted and copied into every snapshot, and never assigned anywhere.

`dynamics` was the fourth, and it was a crest factor, `(peak - average) / peak`, smoothed. It held at 0.65 whether anyone was talking or not, and turning the volume down could not move it, which is the report and is also the proof that the field was measuring the wrong thing. A crest factor is a property of a waveform's shape, and every shape a room produces sits near the same place: speech, hum, noise and music through a small speaker all read about 0.65. A number that cannot move cannot separate a calm room from a busy one, which is what the mood classifier and several layer opacities ask it to do. It is now the span the loudness covers while its window is open, as a fraction of the top of that span. Two remembered edges follow the envelope, quickly outwards and slowly back in, so the window is a few seconds wide. A room holding one level reads near zero, and a passage that goes loud and then quiet reads high. The classifier's dynamics cut points already moved with the observed range, so they follow it without a change, and the harness check that proves the cut point can be crossed in both directions was rewritten, because its fixture was a sine against a single-sample spike, which is the right pair for a crest factor and moves the envelope not at all.

The third thing `level` exposed was in the page rather than in the analysis. Every reference here is a statistic of the input, which is what makes the features gain-independent, and it is also what makes a change of input invisible to them: the analysis cannot see that the samples stopped coming from the same place, so it goes on measuring the new signal against the old one's peak. The page's two sources are about forty times apart, so switching back to the microphone after the synthetic signal had been playing read a fraction of the truth until the reference decayed, some thirteen seconds. Nothing was wrong with the microphone and nothing was wrong with the normalisation. `AudioProcessor::resetTracking` drops the references, `gg_reset_analysis` exports it, and every path in the page that changes the source calls it.
